Three influential empires

Ghana (Wagadu)

c. 6th–13th centuries. A Sahelian power whose core lay around the western Sahel, in areas of today’s southeastern Mauritania and western Mali. Its name is not the same as the modern Republic of Ghana.

Mali

c. 13th–16th centuries. At its height, its influence covered a vast part of West Africa, including the upper Niger region. Timbuktu and Djenné became celebrated centers of trade and learning.

Songhai

c. 15th–16th centuries. A major empire centered on the middle Niger, with its political heart at Gao; it expanded across much of the western Sahel before Moroccan invasion in 1591.

Dates and extents are simplified; historical boundaries shifted and are debated. The colored zones are not precise borders.

Could there be gold or crude oil?

Gold

Gold is already mined in multiple African countries. Well-known belts and producing areas include parts of West Africa (including Ghana, Mali, Burkina Faso, and Côte d’Ivoire), as well as parts of East and Southern Africa. Undiscovered deposits are possible, but only geological mapping, sampling, and drilling can establish them.

Crude oil

Africa has established petroleum provinces, including the Gulf of Guinea, North Africa, and offshore and onshore basins elsewhere, such as Angola. Other areas may have exploration potential, but a basin or surface indication does not prove a commercial discovery. Exploration requires permits and environmental and community safeguards.

Historical connection: Gold and trans-Saharan trade contributed to the wealth of these empires. That history is important, but it does not imply that their former territories contain unrecognized modern deposits.
